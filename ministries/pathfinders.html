Why Pathfinders

Why every kid should join a Pathfinder club

Pathfinders is where children become leaders. Every meeting, campout and honor is built to grow a young person spiritually, mentally, physically and socially, with adults who model what it means to walk with Jesus.

  • church

    A faith of their own

    Pathfinders don't just hear about Jesus. They study the Bible, lead worship and pray with friends until faith becomes something they own.

  • diversity_3

    Friends who last

    A unit of peers and a counselor who knows their name, week after week, through the middle-school years when belonging matters most.

  • forest

    Real skills, outdoors

    Pitching a tent, reading a map, giving first aid, cooking over a fire. Skills that build confidence far from a screen.

  • flag

    Leadership, early

    Pathfinders lead devotions, call drill commands and teach younger members, and older teens train as junior staff.

  • volunteer_activism

    Service to New York

    Food drives, neighborhood clean-ups and outreach across the five boroughs and Long Island teach that faith shows up in action.

  • shield_person

    Adults who invest

    Every staff member is screened and trained. Many were Pathfinders themselves and now give back as mentors.

What a Pathfinder promises

The Pathfinder Pledge

“By the grace of God, I will be pure and kind and true. I will keep the Pathfinder Law. I will be a servant of God and a friend to man.”

The Pledge is the promise at the heart of every club. The Law is how a Pathfinder lives it out, one line at a time.

Dr. Andrew Gordon

Associate Director · Club Ministry

The Pathfinder Law

  1. Keep the morning watch.
  2. Do my honest part.
  3. Care for my body.
  4. Keep a level eye.
  5. Be courteous and obedient.
  6. Walk softly in the sanctuary.
  7. Keep a song in my heart.
  8. Go on God’s errands.

The growth journey

Six classes, one for every year of growing up

The classes are the heart of Pathfinders. Each school year a Pathfinder works through one class, going deeper in faith, skill and service, and is invested before the church when it is complete.

Before PathfindersAdventurers · ages 4–9arrow_forwardPathfinders · six classesarrow_forwardOlder Pathfinders · ages 14–17Teen Leadership TrainingWhat comes nextMaster Guide

  1. Grade 5 · age 10

    Friend

    A friend of Jesus, of nature and of others. The first year: the Pledge and Law, a first campout and first honors.

  2. Grade 6 · age 11

    Companion

    Walking alongside others: learning to serve, to share faith and to be the teammate a unit can count on.

  3. Grade 7 · age 12

    Explorer

    Exploring God's Word and God's world further, with longer hikes, deeper Bible study and new honors.

  4. Grade 8 · age 13

    Ranger

    Self-reliance on the trail and in life: outdoor skill, good choices and care for the people around them.

  5. Grade 9 · age 14

    Voyager

    Going further: mentoring younger Pathfinders, planning service projects and taking a bigger role in the club.

  6. Grade 10 · age 15

    Guide

    Guiding others: leadership that prepares a Pathfinder for Teen Leadership Training and the Master Guide path.

Class A

The Pathfinder uniform

The uniform is a visible announcement of Pathfinder ministry. Every patch and pin on it tells part of a Pathfinder's story.

  • Tan shirt

    Two pleated pockets with flaps and shoulder epaulets; long or short sleeves.

  • Black slacks

    Straight leg, pleated front, belt loops, no cuff.

  • Black skirt

    A-line with a pleated front, hemmed at mid-knee or below.

  • Web belt

    Black, with the Pathfinder buckle.

  • Yellow neckerchief

    Black trim and the black slide; TLTs wear red trim.

  • military_tech

    Black sash

    Over the right shoulder: honors, camporee patches and pins.

  • steps

    Shoes and socks

    All black dress shoes or boots and plain black socks.

  • face

    Beret or cap

    Optional, and the same across the club.

Levels of dress

  • Class AThe full uniform above, for services, parades and investiture.
  • Modified AClass A without the sash, for active days.
  • Class BUniform shirt and neckerchief with jeans.
  • Class CThe club T-shirt with jeans: field uniform for camping and service.

When to wear it

  • Club meetings and special programs: Pathfinder Day, Induction, Investiture
  • Ushers, messengers, color guards and first-aid details
  • Outreach: food baskets, literature, visits to nursing homes

Not for work, play, or selling for personal profit.

For parents

Questions families ask

Answers below are drafts for the Director to confirm.

Does my child have to be Adventist to join?add

No. Pathfinder clubs welcome any child aged 10 to 16 who is willing to take part in the program, including its spiritual activities.

What does it cost?add

Each club sets its own annual fee to cover insurance, class materials and events. Uniforms are purchased separately; many clubs help families with costs. Ask the local director.

When and where do clubs meet?add

Most clubs meet weekly or twice a month at their church, usually Sunday morning or Sabbath afternoon, from September to June.

How are children kept safe?add

Every adult staff member completes a background check and child-protection training before serving, and clubs follow Adventist Risk Management guidelines for trips and camping.

Where do I buy the uniform?add

Class A uniform pieces, sashes and insignia are available through the GNYC Youth Store. Your director will tell you what a new Pathfinder needs first.

Join a club, or start one at your church.

The conference Pathfinder office will connect you with a club near you or walk your church through chartering a new one.
